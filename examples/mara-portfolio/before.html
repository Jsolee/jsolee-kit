02 — About

Structure first, surface second.

Architect by training. Product designer by trade.

  1. Principle 01Fewer, clearer decisions

    Every screen should make the next step obvious — and the wrong one hard.

  2. Principle 02Design the edge cases first

    The happy path is easy. Trust is built on the error states.

  3. Principle 03Ship, then listen

    A tested prototype beats a perfect deck. Real use beats both.

I studied architecture, then spent twelve years inside product teams. The lesson that stuck: most software doesn’t need more features. It needs fewer, clearer decisions.

Today I work with two or three teams a year, usually on the unglamorous screens that carry the most weight — the schedule a nurse opens forty times a shift, the reconciliation table a controller stares at on the last day of the quarter.

I run my own research, write my own specs and stay close to engineering until things ship. You get one senior person, not a pitch team and a hand-off.

How a sprint usually runs

Three weeks, kickoff to handoff
  1. 1Week 1Listen

    Interviews with users, support and sales. I read the tickets nobody reads.

  2. 2Week 1Map

    Flows, edge cases and the decisions hidden inside them, on one wall.

  3. 3Week 2Sketch

    Many rough options, quickly. We pick a direction together.

  4. 4Week 2 – 3Prototype

    A realistic prototype, tested with five to eight real users.
