Dispatch board for a grocery delivery fleet

Novi Sad, 2025 · Lead designer, 5 months

Dispatchers ran the morning wave from three browser tabs and a whiteboard. I rebuilt the board around drivers instead of orders, so moving a delivery is one drag and the vehicle’s load updates while you do it.

The board replays one assignment: an urgent order goes to Ana’s cargo bike, her load bar fills and her route grows on the map.

How a project usually runs

Most projects take ten to fourteen weeks. I work inside your tools, and there is something you can click every Friday, so nobody waits a month to see where it’s going.

A clickable build every Friday

123456789101112

Sit in Weeks 1 to 2

I watch the people who use the tool do their actual work, on calls or on site, and write down every workaround.

Map Weeks 2 to 3

One page with every screen, state and handoff. Disagreements come up here, while they are still cheap.

Prototype Weeks 3 to 6

Clickable prototypes with real data, tested with the same people from week one.

Ship in slices Weeks 6 to 12

I pair with your engineers and review builds, not files. Something reaches real users every week.

Hand over Weeks 11 to 12

Components, states and the reasons behind each decision, written down so the next change doesn’t need me.

About

Before going independent in 2019 I spent six years in‑house, first at a logistics software company in Novi Sad, then at a payments startup in Berlin. Both taught me the same thing: the people who use a product all day know exactly where it hurts, and they are rarely in the room when it gets designed.

So I bring them in. I sit with dispatchers, receptionists and surveyors before I open a design file, and I keep showing them work until it ships.
