Most software can’t spell my name. I’m a product designer, so I take it personally.

I work with one team at a time, and I start with what the brief left out: the driver who drops off the map, the half-filled prescription, the surname that won’t fit the column.

Smena

Control room software for a regional parcel courier. Research on the night shift, flows, interface, design system.

When a driver’s phone went dark, the old board kept a confident green dot at the last known position. Now it says the position is a guess, shows what’s aboard, and puts the two things a dispatcher can do right next to it.

Recept

Repeat prescriptions for a pharmacy chain. Research, app design, prototype.

The brief had two states, ready and not ready. Someone on daily medication needs a third: what can I collect tonight?

Plata

Payroll for small firms. Interface, design system, handoff to engineering.

Real payrolls hold double-barrelled surnames and people who left on the 12th. The table never cuts a name off, and it stops to ask about whatever it can’t work out alone.

Knjiga

Bookkeeping for freelancers. Onboarding and first run.

Day one is the only screen every customer sees, and the brief didn’t have it. Instead of sample data that looks like someone else’s business, the empty account offers two ways in.

I’ve spent my working life inside product teams and went independent to stay close to the work. I take one client at a time, join your standup and your Slack, and work in whatever your engineers already open. You get the same person from the first messy doc to the last handoff note.

Good fit
