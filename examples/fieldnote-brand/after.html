Brand sheet, first edition, October 2026

A level meter on its side
is a paragraph.

Fieldnote records the meeting and writes the follow-up email. The mark is that one move: four lines of sound, set flush left and cut to an F. The last line ends on the record light, which stays on the page as a full stop.

  1. A level meter. The meeting as sound, read left to right.

  2. Turned 90° clockwise. Now it reads top to bottom, as lines of text.

  3. The light that said recording ends the note as a full stop.

Three ideas, in black

Form first, color after. Each one starts from something the product does.

The winner, tested

Every bar sits on a 4-unit grid, so the mark lands on whole pixels at 60, 30 and 15 px tall.

True size: 60, 30, 15 px tall, then beside the wordmark.

Mark

Four lines of 48, 20, 36 and 20 units. The stop is 13 across, one more than a bar, because a circle has to be larger to look the same size. Its gap to the stem is 3.5, not 4, for the same reason.

Wordmark

Drawn, not typed. Its x-height lands on the top of the mark's second bar and the crossbars of f and t line up with it. The tittle on the i is square, so the stop is the only round dot in the name.

Minimum size

  • Mark, 15 px tall
  • Lockup, 14 px tall
  • Wordmark, 14 px tall

One accent, four neutrals

Widths show roughly how much of each to use. Blaze comes from surveyors' flagging tape and does two jobs: the record light, and the one primary action on a screen. Everything else is neutral.

Do

  • Use the mark alone where the name is already in view: tab, dock, avatar.
  • Show Blaze once per surface. If an action needs it, the mark goes one color.
  • Treat the dot as a promise. It pulses only while recording: 1.2 s, ease-out. With reduced motion it holds still and the word Recording does the work. This sheet plays it once on load, as a demo.
